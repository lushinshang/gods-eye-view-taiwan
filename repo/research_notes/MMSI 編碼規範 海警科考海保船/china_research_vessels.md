# Chinese Government/Scientific Research Vessel MMSI Numbering Patterns

## For specific named Chinese research vessels, what are their documented MMSI numbers, and do they share a common prefix pattern beyond the generic China MID block?

### Takeaway
Specific MMSI-to-vessel mappings were found for most named vessels via third-party AIS aggregators (MarineTraffic/VesselFinder/MagicPort/Maritime Optima), but no evidence of a distinct sub-range for research/government vessels beyond China's three generic Maritime Identification Digits (MIDs): 412, 413, 414. Several vessels also show **multiple conflicting MMSI numbers across sources/time**, which is itself a notable data-quality finding, not a pattern.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- All documented MMSIs fall under China's generic MIDs 412/413/414, identical to the block used by ordinary Chinese merchant and fishing vessels. No evidence surfaced of a distinct numeric sub-range, prefix, or "government/research" tier within 412/413/414 — civilian research vessels appear to draw from the same national MMSI pool as any other China-flagged ship.
- The recurring MMSI conflicts across sources for the same IMO (Xue Long, Shiyan 3, Dayang Yihao) suggest either (a) MMSI reassignment over the vessel's service life, (b) aggregator database errors/stale caching, or (c) AIS-transmitted MMSI changes (possibly deliberate) — this research could not distinguish between these explanations with the sources available.

### Gaps
- No live/current-snapshot MarineTraffic page was retrieved directly (only MarineTraffic-adjacent aggregators); a direct check of MarineTraffic.com and an authoritative registry (e.g. ITU MARS database) would be needed to resolve the Xue Long, Shiyan 3, and Dayang Yihao MMSI conflicts.
- Could not determine why multiple MMSIs are associated with the same IMO for three vessels — no source explained vessel MMSI reassignment history for China-flagged ships specifically.

## Is there a known distinction in Chinese MMSI allocation between ordinary merchant/fishing vessels and state-owned research/government vessels, per official PRC regulation (中国海事局 MMSI分配规则)?

### Takeaway
No official Chinese Ministry of Transport (MOT) / China Maritime Safety Administration (MSA) document describing a distinct MMSI allocation category for research or government vessels was found. Chinese regulatory documents located instead concern the separate "船舶識別號" (ship identification number) system, not MMSI, and general ITU-R M.585 guidance governs MMSI structure without a China-specific carve-out for government/research vessels.

### Cited Findings
- Search for 中国海事局 MMSI 分配规则 returned no MSA-published MMSI allocation methodology; results were limited to ITU general MMSI/MID background documents — [ITU-R M.585-10](https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/m/R-REC-M.585-10-202604-I!!MSW-E.docx), [Wikipedia MMSI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_Mobile_Service_Identity).
- Under ITU-R M.585, an MMSI is a 9-digit identifier issued by a vessel's flag state; MID digits (three) identify the country, and Annex 3 of M.585 gives general administrative guidance on assignment, management, conservation, and reuse of identity resources (including reuse of assignments with trailing zeros), with no vessel-category distinction specified — [ITU-R M.585 family](https://itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/m/R-REC-M.585-5-200910-S!!PDF-C.pdf).
- WRC-23 ITU documentation notes M.585-8 raised the cap to up to 1,000,000 MMSI numbers per MID for ship stations — [ITU WRC-23 report](https://www.itu.int/dms_pub/itu-r/md/23/wrc23/c/R23-WRC23-C-0004!A4!MSW-C.docx). No per-MID breakdown by vessel category (government vs. commercial) was retrievable from the extracted text.
- Search for msa.gov.cn MMSI application/allocation rules instead surfaced Chinese Ministry of Transport (交通运输部) documents governing the **船舶識別號 (ship identification number)** — a distinct identifier issued under the Ship Registration Regulations, applied for at local maritime authorities and reviewed/granted by China MSA within 2 working days — [MOT 2023 administrative document](https://xxgk.mot.gov.cn/2020/xzgfxwj/202304/W020230428378498672472.pdf); [MOT notice on review process](https://xxgk.mot.gov.cn/2020/gz/202112/W020220809378199906274.pdf). This is explicitly a different identifier from MMSI and the documents do not describe MMSI issuance procedure.

### Inferences
- The absence of any located PRC document distinguishing MMSI allocation for research/government vessels, combined with the fact that documented research-vessel MMSIs sit in the same 412/413/414 ranges as ordinary shipping, suggests China does not operate a separate MMSI tier for state research vessels — but this is an absence-of-evidence inference, not a confirmed negative.

### Gaps
- Could not access msa.gov.cn directly to check for an MMSI-specific (as opposed to ship-identification-number) application procedure; the located MOT documents govern a different identifier system. A targeted search in Chinese for "海事局 MMSI 申请" or direct navigation of msa.gov.cn's radio/GMDSS licensing section would be the next step.
- No source was found confirming or denying whether China's MSA uses internal (non-public) sub-blocks within MID 412/413/414 for state-owned vessels; this would likely only be visible through statistical analysis of a large MMSI dataset, which was outside the scope of searches performed.

## Have Chinese research vessels been the subject of maritime security reporting on operations in Taiwan/Japan/South China Sea EEZs, including AIS manipulation or "dark" periods?

### Takeaway
Multiple documented incidents exist of Chinese research vessels operating in neighboring states' EEZs (Vietnam, Philippines, Indonesia, India's Andaman waters, and near Taiwan/Japan), with several specific, named instances of AIS anomalies (false broadcast positions, claimed malfunctions, and "going dark"), though a confirmed, sourced AIS-off incident specifically inside Taiwan's own EEZ was not found in this search.

### Cited Findings
- **Jia Hai Ke 7 (佳海科7, Sept–Oct 2026):** Philippine Coast Guard said the Chinese research ship was broadcasting false AIS positions while loitering ~38 nautical miles northwest of Itbayat, Batanes (~160km from Taiwan) — [Baird Maritime](https://www.bairdmaritime.com/work-boat-world/research-environment-training/philippines-catches-chinese-research-ship-faking-location-near-taiwan); [Interaksyon/Philstar](https://interaksyon.philstar.com/politics-issues/2026/09/28/319823/philippines-accuses-chinese-vessel-of-spoofing-location-in-northern-waters/). Taiwan's coast guard was separately reported monitoring the same vessel with three ships — [Tribune.net.ph](https://tribune.net.ph/2026/10/03/taiwan-coast-guard-also-monitoring-chinese-research-vessel-off-batanes). China's embassy disputed the Philippine characterization, saying the vessel was operating normally in waters under China's jurisdiction — [Taipei Times](https://www.taipeitimes.com/News/front/archives/2026/10/02/2003865248).
- **Xiang Yang Hong 22 (June 2026):** Taiwan's coast guard warned the vessel out of restricted waters off Su'ao on Taiwan's east coast, after first warning it while in Taiwan's EEZ; no AIS-specific detail reported in this incident — [Focus Taiwan](https://focustaiwan.tw/politics/202606190010).
- **Xiang Yang Hong 03 (January 2021, Indonesia):** Indonesia reported the vessel claimed its AIS had "malfunctioned" while operating in Indonesian waters — [Safety4Sea](https://safety4sea.com/indonesia-spots-chinese-research-vessel-with-tracking-system-off/).
- **Xiang Yang Hong 01 (undated, reported via Sealight/commentary, near Indonesian waters):** Described as "documented switching off its AIS transponder near Indonesian waters," which "raised diplomatic scrutiny" — [Sealight gray-zone tactics playbook](https://www.sealight.live/posts/gray-zone-tactics-playbook-intrusive-surveys). The playbook's own source for this specific claim was not cited in the retrieved text, so it should be treated as a secondary/unverified claim pending a primary source.
- **Xiang Yang Hong 01 + Dong Fang Hong 3 (February 2025):** Reported operating near India's Andaman and Nicobar Islands during Indian ballistic missile tests, raising covert-surveillance concerns; no AIS behavior reported for this specific episode — [Sealight gray-zone tactics playbook](https://www.sealight.live/posts/gray-zone-tactics-playbook-intrusive-surveys).
- **Xiang Yang Hong 10 (2023, Vietnam) and Xiang Yang Hong 18 (October 2023, near the Senkaku/Diaoyu Islands):** Both cited as incidents by the same Sealight playbook, but described without AIS-specific detail in the retrieved excerpt — [Sealight gray-zone tactics playbook](https://www.sealight.live/posts/gray-zone-tactics-playbook-intrusive-surveys).
- **Shi Yan 1 (2019, India's Andaman and Nicobar Islands / Port Blair):** Detected operating off Port Blair — [H I Sutton, hisutton.com](https://www.hisutton.com/Chinese-Survey-SCS.html). In May 2020 it surveyed the mid-South China Sea in a ~60nm-diameter circular pattern with smaller searches in the center; no AIS-off behavior reported for this vessel in this source.
- **Hai Yang Di Zhi 8 (2019, Vietnam's EEZ):** Conducted an extensive survey inside Vietnam's EEZ, prompting a Vietnamese diplomatic protest, escorted by 14 vessels believed to be Chinese Maritime Militia, periodically stopping at Fiery Cross Reef (Chinese-controlled) — [H I Sutton, hisutton.com](https://www.hisutton.com/Chinese-Survey-SCS.html). In 2020 it surveyed a smaller area near Hainan with far fewer escort vessels.
- **General pattern claim (unverified/general, not a specific incident):** The Sealight playbook states Chinese research vessels "may disable their AIS transponders ('going dark') before sensitive operations" — stated generally, without a specific vessel/date attached — [Sealight gray-zone tactics playbook](https://www.sealight.live/posts/gray-zone-tactics-playbook-intrusive-surveys). This should be treated as a general/speculative pattern claim, distinct from the specific verified incidents above.
- A Philippine military official separately stated a research vessel operating in eastern Philippine waters had turned off its AIS tracking system (vessel name not resolved in the search snippet) — [Inquirer GlobalNation](https://GLOBALNATION.inquirer.net/234375/research-vessel-in-eastern-ph-turns-off-tracking-system-ph-military).

### Inferences
- Across the Philippines, Vietnam, and Indonesia cases, a recurring pattern emerges of Chinese research vessels either going AIS-dark, claiming AIS "malfunction," or broadcasting false positions while operating inside or near other states' EEZs — consistent across three separate national coast guards' reporting, which strengthens credibility despite each individual incident coming from a single primary source.
- No independently sourced, dated incident of AIS manipulation occurring specifically within Taiwan's own claimed EEZ was found; the Taiwan-adjacent cases found (Jia Hai Ke 7, Xiang Yang Hong 22) either occurred in the Philippines' EEZ (with Taiwan monitoring from outside) or did not include AIS-specific allegations.

### Gaps
- Could not confirm a primary source (e.g. an original Indonesian, Philippine, or Taiwanese government statement, or a named AIS-tracking methodology) for the Sealight playbook's claim that Xiang Yang Hong 01 was "documented" turning off AIS near Indonesia — this claim rests on a secondary commentary source only.
- Could not identify the specific vessel name in the Inquirer/GlobalNation report of a research vessel turning off AIS in eastern Philippine waters; the search snippet did not resolve this.
- No search was performed specifically for Japan/Senkaku AIS-off incidents beyond the one Xiang Yang Hong 18 mention; a dedicated search in Japanese or English news (e.g. "尖閣 中国 調査船 AIS") would likely surface more Japan-specific detail and was not completed within the tool-call budget for this assignment.

# ITU MID (Maritime Identification Digits) Official Allocation Rules — China / Japan / General Structure

## Q1: What is the complete, exact list of MID codes currently allocated to the People's Republic of China (中國)? Is it really 412-416?

### Takeaway
Across all sources found, the People's Republic of China is allocated **MID 412, 413, 414** — a block of three, not five. MID 415 appears in no source as allocated to anyone. MID 416 is listed separately under the ITU table's politically-framed entry "China (People's Republic of) - Taiwan (Province of China)" — i.e. in the ITU's own table it is bundled under the PRC's sovereign name-string but functions as a distinct MID used for vessels registered in Taiwan, not as a fourth/fifth working MID of mainland China's fleet. The common claim "412-416 全部屬於中國" is **not accurate**: only 412-414 are confirmed as mainland China's MMSI-issuing MIDs across independent transcriptions; 416 is the Taiwan-labeled entry, and 415 could not be confirmed as allocated at all.

### Cited Findings
- A direct transcription of the ITU's own "Table of Maritime Identification Digits" spreadsheet (file `MaritimeIdentificationDigits-...xlsx`, downloaded from itu.int) lists: 412 → China (People's Republic of); 413 → China (People's Republic of); 414 → China (People's Republic of); 415 → no entry; 416 → "China (People's Republic of) - Taiwan (Province of China)" — [Jordan Bell, ITU Table of Maritime Identification Digits transcription, 2023-05-29](http://jordanbell.info/blog/2023/05/29/ITU-Table-of-Maritime-Identification-Digits.html)
- Wikipedia's "Maritime identification digits" article (sourced, per its own citation, from an ITU web page "Table of Maritime Identification Digits," itu.int, ITU-R terrestrial section, as retrieved 2020-02-04) lists China (People's Republic of China) under MID 412, 413, 414, and lists Taiwan separately (Republic of China flag) under MID 416 only, with no explanatory note on the political framing — [Maritime identification digits – Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_identification_digits)
- A third independent secondary compilation (siranah.de) likewise lists China (People's Republic of China) with MID 412, 413 as the headline entries (this source's fetched excerpt did not show 414, but is consistent with 412/413 as PRC codes; it does not attribute a specific ITU document number) — [siranah.de MID/Country table](https://siranah.de/html/sail018s.htm)
- An earlier, less precise web-search summary (not independently re-verified) claimed 412, 413, and **416** as mainland China and 414/415 as other Southeast/South Asian countries — this conflicts with the two transcriptions above and could not be confirmed as accurate; it is flagged here only to show that conflicting claims exist in circulation. — contradicted by the two primary transcriptions cited above
- The legal basis for MID allocation is ITU Radio Regulations (RR) No. 19.108A (§41): "The maritime identification digits M1I2D3 ... denote, in principle, the administration responsible for the station so identified. In some cases, M1I2D3 may denote a geographical area under the responsibility of a specific administration... some maritime identification digits are reserved for maritime devices and do not correspond either to an administration or to a geographical area." (WRC-15 revision) — [ITU Radio Regulations, Article 19, Section VI (fxm-art19-sec6.pdf)](https://itu.int/en/ITU-R/terrestrial/fmd/Documents/fxm-art19-sec6.pdf)
- RR No. 20.16 requires the ITU Secretary-General / Radiocommunication Bureau to maintain and regularly publish the allocated-MID list; the authoritative, continuously updated version lives in the ITU MARS (Maritime mobile Access and Retrieval System) database and in ITU publication **List V — List of Ship Stations and Maritime Mobile Service Identity Assignments** (most recent editions found: 2022, 2023, 2026) — [ITU List V – List of Ship Stations and MMSI Assignments (2026 ed.)](https://www.itu.int/hub/publication/r-sp-lm-v-2026/); [MARS portal](https://www.itu.int/en/ITU-R/terrestrial/mars/Pages/shipstationslist.aspx)

### Inferences
- Because MID allocation can change over time (RR No. 20.16 process; administrations can request additional MIDs once they exhaust >80% of an existing block — see ITU-R M.585-10 §"Additional MID" provisions, Q3 below), a definitive "as of today" answer requires a direct pull from the live ITU MARS database or the current List V/List IV edition, not a secondary transcription. The three convergent secondary sources (Wikipedia, Jordan Bell's spreadsheet transcription, siranah.de) agreeing on 412-414 for PRC gives reasonable confidence, but this report's confidence is secondary-source-level, not a primary-document screenshot.
- The "412-416" figure commonly circulated likely originated from loosely merging the PRC block (412-414) with the Taiwan-labeled 416 entry (and possibly a stray reference to 415), rather than reflecting a genuine 5-code PRC allocation.

### Gaps
- Could not directly open/extract the ITU's live MARS MID table or the current List V/List IV PDF table text (PDF text extraction attempts on the official ITU documents either failed PDF parsing or returned only unrelated front-matter/abbreviation tables, not the actual Table 3 MID rows). The findings above rest on secondary transcriptions/mirrors of that official table, not on this researcher directly reading ITU's Table 3 rows.
- Could not confirm whether MID 415 is genuinely unassigned, reserved, or assigned to a country not captured by the sources checked.
- Could not confirm the exact current publication date/edition of the live MID table (MARS is continuously updated; the static mirrors found carry a 2020 or 2023 retrieval date, so a 2026-dated re-check against the live ITU MARS portal is recommended before treating 412-414/416 as current).

## Q2: What is the complete, exact list of MID codes currently allocated to Japan (日本)? Is it really 431-432?

### Takeaway

### Cited Findings

## Q3: According to ITU-R M.585 and related ITU documents, is there a formally defined digit-pattern distinction for different categories of maritime users within a country's MID block? Quote the exact digit template rules.

### Takeaway
Yes — ITU-R Recommendation M.585 (current edition: **M.585-10, dated April 2026**; the version referenced by the current ITU Radio Regulations text found was **M.585-8**) formally and precisely defines distinct 9-digit MMSI templates for each category of maritime user: ship station, group ship station, coast station (with five optional sub-category patterns), group coast station, SAR aircraft (with two optional sub-category patterns), AIS aids-to-navigation (with three optional sub-category patterns), craft associated with a parent ship, handheld VHF/DSC transceivers, and several special-purpose device identities (AIS-SART, MOB-AIS, EPIRB-AIS, AMRD Group A/B). Full exact templates below, extracted directly from the primary-source PDF text of Rec. ITU-R M.585-10.

### Cited Findings
- **Document identity confirmed from the PDF itself:** "Recommendation ITU-R M.585-10 (04/2026) Assignment and use of identities in the maritime mobile service," published by the ITU Radiocommunication Bureau (BR) — [Rec. ITU-R M.585-10 PDF](https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/m/R-REC-M.585-10-202604-I!!PDF-E.pdf)
- **Ship station** (Annex 1, Section 1, para 1): format `M₁I₂D₃X₄X₅X₆X₇X₈X₉` — first three digits are the MID, X = any digit 0-9. "The MID denotes the administration having jurisdiction over the ship station so identified." — Rec. ITU-R M.585-10, Annex 1 §1.1
- **Group ship station** (Annex 1, Section 1, para 4): format `01M₂I₃D₄X₅X₆X₇X₈X₉` — first digit is 0, second is 1, then MID, then free digits. "The MID represents only the territory or geographical area of the administration assigning the group ship station call identity and does not therefore prevent group calls to fleets containing more than one ship nationality." — Rec. ITU-R M.585-10, Annex 1 §1.4
- **Coast station** (Annex 1, Section 2, para 1): format `0₁0₂M₃I₄D₅X₆X₇X₈X₉` i.e. **00MIDXXXX** — digits 3,4,5 = MID. "The MID reflects the administration having jurisdiction over the coast station or coast earth station." — Rec. ITU-R M.585-10, Annex 1 §2.1
- **Coast station optional 6th-digit sub-categories** (Annex 1, Section 2, para 3), explicitly labeled "example applications," i.e. optional/guidance, not mandatory:
  - `00MID1XXX` — Coast stations
  - `00MID2XXX` — Port stations (harbour radio stations)
  - `00MID3XXX` — Pilot stations
  - `00MID4XXX` — AIS repeater stations
  - `00MID5XXX` — AIS base stations (VDL controlling stations)
  — "This format scheme creates blocks of 999 numbers for each category of station, however the method is optional and should be used only as a guidance." — Rec. ITU-R M.585-10, Annex 1 §2.3-2.4
- **Group coast station** (Annex 1, Section 2, para 5-6): format `00MIDXXXX` subset where first two figures are zero; the specific combination `00MID00000` ("0102M3I4D506070809") is reserved as the Group Coast Station Identity addressing all 00MIDXXXX stations within the administration — Rec. ITU-R M.585-10, Annex 1 §2.5-2.6
- **All-coast-stations identity**: the fixed combination `010293949506070809` (i.e. a specific all-9/structured value) is reserved to address all VHF `00XXXXXXX` stations; not applicable to MF/HF coast stations — Rec. ITU-R M.585-10, Annex 1 §2.8
- **SAR aircraft** (Annex 1, Section 3, para 1): format `1₁1₂1₃M₄I₅D₆X₇X₈X₉` i.e. **111MIDXXX** — digits 4,5,6 = MID. "The MID represents the administration having jurisdiction over the aircraft call identity." Accommodates 999 aircraft per MID; if exceeded, administration may request an additional MID. — Rec. ITU-R M.585-10, Annex 1 §3.1-3.2
- **SAR aircraft optional 7th-digit sub-categories** (Annex 1, Section 3, para 3), again explicitly "optional":
  - `111MID1XX` — Fixed-wing aircraft
  - `111MID5XX` — Helicopters
  — Rec. ITU-R M.585-10, Annex 1 §3.3
- **Group Aircraft Identity**: fixed combination `111MID000` ("111213M4I5D6070809") reserved to address all `111MIDXXX` stations of the administration — Rec. ITU-R M.585-10, Annex 1 §3.5
- **AIS aids to navigation (AtoN)** (Annex 1, Section 4, para 1): format `9₁9₂M₃I₄D₅X₆X₇X₈X₉` i.e. **99MIDXXXX** — digits 3,4,5 = MID. "The MID represents the administration having jurisdiction over the call identity for the navigational aid." Applies to all AtoN types per the AIS Message 21 "Type of aids to navigation" parameter in Rec. ITU-R M.1371. Accommodates 10,000 AtoN per MID. — Rec. ITU-R M.585-10, Annex 1 §4.1-4.3
- **AtoN optional 6th-digit sub-categories** (Annex 1, Section 4, para 5), explicitly "optional... should be used only as a guidance":
  - `99MID1XXX` — Physical AIS AtoN
  - `99MID6XXX` — Virtual AIS AtoN
  - `99MID8XXX` — Mobile AtoN
  — Rec. ITU-R M.585-10, Annex 1 §4.5. Note: the 7th digit "may be used for national purposes, to define areas where the AIS AtoN are located or types of AIS AtoN to the discretion of the administration concerned." (§4.7)
- **Craft associated with a parent ship** (Annex 1, Section 5, para 1): format `9₁8₂M₃I₄D₅X₆X₇X₈X₉` i.e. **98MIDXXXX** — digits 3,4,5 = MID. Covers devices on lifeboats/life-rafts/rescue-boats and first-/second-generation EPIRBs associated with a parent ship (explicitly excludes AIS-SART). Each craft gets a unique MMSI separately registered/linked to the parent ship's MMSI. Accommodates 10,000 per MID. — Rec. ITU-R M.585-10, Annex 1 §5.1-5.4
- **Handheld VHF transceivers with DSC + integral GNSS** (Annex 2, Section 1): format `8₁M₂I₃D₄X₅X₆X₇X₈X₉` i.e. **8MIDXXXXX** — digits 2,3,4 = MID. Accommodates 100,000 per MID. — Rec. ITU-R M.585-10, Annex 2 §1 (surrounding context: para numbered "accommodate 100 000 VHF transceivers per MID")
- **Special-purpose devices** (Annex 2, later sections): 9-digit identities with fixed 3-digit prefixes — **970** = AIS-SART, **972** = MOB (man overboard) device, **974** = EPIRB-AIS, **979** = AMRD (Autonomous Maritime Radio Device) Group B. AMRD Group B identities "should be limited to the MID 979" specifically (979 functions as a reserved pseudo-MID for this device class rather than a country code). — Rec. ITU-R M.585-10, Annex 2 (prefix list paragraph) and §"Autonomous maritime radio devices Group B"
- **Legal/regulatory basis in the ITU Radio Regulations**: RR No. 19.99 (§39, WRC-19 revision) requires administrations to assign MMSI "in accordance with the provisions described in Annex 1 of Recommendation ITU-R M.585-8" (note: RR text found cites M.585-8; the Recommendation itself has since been updated to M.585-10, April 2026 — the RR cross-reference is periodically updated at WRC cycles and may lag the Recommendation's own version number). RR No. 19.102 (§40(3)) states "The types of maritime mobile service identities shall be as described in Annex 1 of Recommendation ITU-R M.585-8." RR No. 19.108A (§41) is the core MID definition clause (quoted in Q1). — [ITU Radio Regulations, Article 19, Section VI](https://itu.int/en/ITU-R/terrestrial/fmd/Documents/fxm-art19-sec6.pdf)
- **MID reuse/exhaustion rule**: ITU-R M.585-10 states administrations should avoid depleting their MID/MMSI series, and "when an administration determines it has a need for additional allocation of a MID, because it has exhausted more than 80% of its allocated MID resource, it should communicate" a request for an additional MID — Rec. ITU-R M.585-10, Annex (final section on MID conservation/additional allocation)

### Inferences
- The sub-category digit patterns (coast station 00MID1XXX/2XXX/etc., SAR aircraft 111MID1XX/5XX, AtoN 99MID1XXX/6XXX/8XXX) are explicitly described by the Recommendation itself as "optional," "example applications," and "guidance only" — not mandatory ITU-enforced categories. This means an outside observer cannot reliably infer a vessel's official category purely from the digit pattern unless the specific administration is known to actually follow this optional scheme; the Recommendation leaves room for administrations to deviate ("Many other possibilities exist if the administration concerned wishes to augment the scheme").
- The craft-associated-with-parent-ship prefix "98" (as commonly cited, e.g. in the task's suggested patterns) matches the primary text's `9182...` format exactly (98MIDXXXXX), confirming that citation as accurate per the current Recommendation.
- The AtoN prefix "99" matches the primary text exactly (99MIDXXXX), confirming that citation as accurate.
- The SAR aircraft prefix "111" matches the primary text exactly (111MIDXXX), confirming that citation as accurate.
- The coast station prefix "00" matches the primary text exactly (00MIDXXXXX), confirming the task's suggested citation as accurate (note: coast station format has 4 free digits after MID — 00MIDXXXX — not 5; i.e. the full 9-digit identity is 0+0+MID(3)+XXXX(4) = 9 digits).

### Gaps
- None significant for this question — the primary-source PDF text (Rec. ITU-R M.585-10, April 2026 edition) was successfully extracted and quoted directly above via local PDF text extraction, so this answer rests on the primary ITU document itself, not a secondary paraphrase.
- Could not determine the exact WRC/RR cross-reference update status — i.e., whether the ITU Radio Regulations' Article 19 text has been updated post-WRC-23/WRC-27 to cite "M.585-10" instead of the "M.585-8" seen in the Article 19 PDF fetched in this session. This is a minor administrative-lag point, not a substantive gap in the digit-format rules themselves.

## Q4: Is there any officially documented category within MMSI structure specifically for "government vessel" or "law enforcement vessel," distinct from ordinary merchant/fishing vessels, in ITU or IMO AIS standards?

### Takeaway
**No such category exists within the MMSI/MID numbering structure itself** (ITU-R M.585). The MMSI format has no digit pattern that flags a vessel as "government" or "law enforcement" — government, law-enforcement, and coast-guard vessels normally receive an ordinary ship-station MMSI (`MIDXXXXXX`) from their flag administration's MID block, identical in format to any merchant or fishing vessel. However, a **separate AIS data field** — not part of the MMSI — does carry a "Law enforcement vessels" category: the "Type of ship and cargo" parameter defined in **ITU-R Recommendation M.1371** (the AIS technical standard, distinct from M.585 which governs identity *numbering*), transmitted in AIS Message 5 static/voyage data, includes code **55 = "Law enforcement vessels"** within the 50-59 "special craft" range.

### Cited Findings
- A full-text search of the extracted Rec. ITU-R M.585-10 document (covering ship stations, coast stations, SAR aircraft, AtoN, craft associated with a parent ship, handheld transceivers, AIS-SART/MOB/EPIRB/AMRD) found **zero occurrences** of "government," "law enforcement," "military," "warship," or "coast guard" — confirming M.585 defines no such MMSI-structural category. — Rec. ITU-R M.585-10, Annex 1 and Annex 2 (full text search by this researcher)
- Secondary/vendor-level sources (not ITU primary, but describing the ITU-R M.1371 AIS ship-type field) consistently report code **55 = Law enforcement vessels**, within the "special craft" block (50 = Pilot vessel, 51 = SAR vessel, 52 = Tug, 53 = Port tender, 54 = Anti-pollution equipment, 55 = Law enforcement vessels, 56-57 = spare for local use, 58 = Medical transport, 59 = "Noncombatant ship according to RR Resolution No. 18") — [USCG Navigation Center, AIS vessel type codes (attributed to ITU-R M.1371-5)](https://navcen.uscg.gov/node/85); corroborated by [USCG AIS Encoding Guide, Pacific Area](https://www.pacificarea.uscg.mil/Portals/8/District_13/dpw/docs/USCG_AIS_Encoding_Guide-150708.pdf) and a Norwegian AIS reference ([Barentswatch AIS ship type documentation](https://developer.barentswatch.no/docs/AIS/aisshiptype))
- This researcher could not retrieve the ITU's own English-language Rec. ITU-R M.1371-5 PDF text directly (the fetched ITU PDF link returned French-language content); the "code 55 = law enforcement" finding above therefore rests on secondary USCG/vendor reproductions attributing the table to M.1371-5, not on a directly-read ITU primary-source page, per this search session.
- Code 59 is separately notable: it is attributed to "Noncombatant ship according to RR Resolution No. 18," i.e. a category tied to an ITU Radio Regulations Resolution — but this is still the *ship-type/cargo field*, not the MMSI number itself.

### Inferences
- The distinction matters: "government vessel" identification in AIS, where it exists at all in the ITU/IMO framework, is carried by the *static voyage-data ship-type code* (M.1371, field "Type of ship and cargo," value 55 or 59), which is self-declared by the vessel/operator and can be left blank, set incorrectly, or spoofed — it is not cryptographically or structurally tied to the MMSI/MID allocation the way the country identifier is. This is a materially different and much weaker form of "marking" than a dedicated MID/MMSI prefix would be.
- Because no MID/MMSI-level "government vessel" category exists, any claim that a specific MMSI number or prefix (beyond the ordinary ship-station format `MIDXXXXXX`) identifies a vessel as a government/law-enforcement/military ship, absent corroboration from the declared AIS ship-type field or from a flag-state vessel registry, is not supported by the ITU/IMO AIS identity standards reviewed here.

### Gaps
- Could not directly confirm the code-55/"law enforcement vessels" definition against the ITU's own English-language Rec. ITU-R M.1371-5 (or current M.1371) text; this finding rests on secondary USCG and vendor documentation that attributes the table to that Recommendation, not on this researcher's own read of the ITU primary source.
- Could not determine whether a more recent M.1371 revision has changed, renamed, or added further granularity to the ship-type code table (e.g. distinguishing "coast guard" from "law enforcement" from "military/government" non-combatant), since the primary ITU M.1371 text was not successfully retrieved in English in this session.
